How it works

Two minutes a week. Eighteen years of them.

1

Answer one question

Every week you get one question, like "What made her laugh this week?" Write it, say it in a voice note, or record a quick video of yourself. Once a month it's a letter instead.

2

The vault fills up

Your answers, their firsts, the daft things they say and the letters you write all land in their own vault, sorted by age.

3

Hand it over

On their 18th, or a day you choose, they get the key to everything. Sealed letters open when they're old enough.

What's inside

Everything they'll wish they could remember

A letter every month

In their birthday month, a birthday letter sealed until they're 18, so they open one from every year. The rest of the year, "open when" letters for the hard days: when they're scared, heartbroken, leaving home.

Voice of Dad

Your voice reading their bedtime story, telling them about your day. The thing they'll treasure most at 30.

Things they said

The words they get wrong and the questions that floor you. You think you'll remember. You won't.

Firsts

First word, first steps, first day at nursery, first goal. The dates and the details.

The birthday interview

The same ten questions every birthday. Watch the answers change from 3 to 18.

Achievements

Certificates, medals, swimming badges, school reports and drawings, kept long after the fridge door is full.

Privacy

Private by design. Yours for good.

This is a family keepsake, not a social network. Love, Dad isn't open yet. These are the promises we're building it to keep.

  • No feed, no followers, no public sharing. Ever.
  • We're building it so your vault is locked with a key only your family holds. The aim is that not even we can read it.
  • Download everything, any time. It's yours.
  • Name someone you trust to look after the vault if anything happens to you.

Why I'm building this

The weeks were flying. I didn't want to lose any of them.

Photo apps are built for sharing. I wanted something built for keeping: a place for the things I'd want him to know, in my own words, waiting for him when he's old enough to want them.

From the founder

Questions

Fair questions

Do I have to upload photos of my child?

No. Letters, voice notes, videos of you, and written memories are the heart of it. Photos are there if you want them.

Will this still exist when my child is 18?

That's the plan, and you shouldn't have to take our word for it. You can download everything at any time, and the printed yearbooks are your backup on paper.

What if I miss a month's letter?

Nothing breaks. The next month brings the next letter, and you can go back and write a missed one whenever you like.

Can my partner or the grandparents add things?

Yes. Invite them to add memories or write letters. You decide what each person can do.

What happens if something happens to me?

You can name a trusted person to look after the vault and hand it over on the date you've set.

Is it only for dads?

It's written for dads, because almost nothing else is. Mums are welcome to use it too.
